# Gridlock

Original browser RTS (working title). **Not affiliated with EA.**

## Milestone 2

Menu, lobby, then a match: deploy the **Rig** into a **Core**, build **Dynamo / Smelter / Muster / Armory / Airfield / CIWS / RAM / Bunker / Watch Tower / Research Facility**, harvest **scrap** with **Maulers**, train **Troopers**, **Gunners**, **Snipers**, **Rocketers**, **Pyros**, **Mortarmen**, **Medics**, **Drone Ops**, **Jump Jets**, **Walkers**, **Cyborgs**, **Titans**, **Mammoths**, **Nebelwerfers**, **Tigers**, **Apocalypses**, **StuG IIIs**, **Stukas**, **Fw 190s**, and **BV 222s**, and fight (halt-to-fire). Radar lives in the right command bar. Tigers, Apocalypses, Cyborgs, Titans, Mammoths, Nebelwerfers, Drone Ops, and Jump Jets also need a standing **Research Facility**; lose it and those cameos lock again (jobs already queued keep going).

The **Airfield** trains **Stuka** dive bombers, **Fw 190** fighters, and **BV 222** transports and parks up to four in the sandbagged hardstands beside its long strip. A plane taxis out onto the strip and takes off on any order; coming home it lands from the far end, rolls out, and taxis back into its revetment. In the air it flies straight over hills, trees, and water, and circles the point it was sent to. On attack it dives, drops one SC 250 (a direct hit goes through a tank roof; a near miss throws tracks and kills infantry in the burst), strafes soft targets with its wing MGs, then climbs out and comes round again. It flies home by itself when the bomb and belts are spent or the fuel runs low (**Return**, or right-click your Airfield, sends it early), then refuels, rearms, and patches up on the pad. With no Airfield left it circles until the tank is dry and goes down. Only small arms reach a plane in the air from the ground — rifles, machine guns, the PTRD, and the Walker — at a wide spread. Tank guns and mortars cannot. A parked plane is an ordinary ground target.

The **Fw 190** carries no bomb: two 30 mm cannon, one under each wing, and three barrages a sortie. It is faster and turns tighter than the Stuka. On a ground target it dives, and when the target is close and on the nose it looses one barrage: both wings at once, two straight parallel lines of rounds laid along the bearing to the target, running from short of it to past it. Then it flies out, comes round, and fires the next; after the third it goes home to rearm. Fired from above, the rounds come down on a hull's roof (about a third of its side plate), so even the heaviest tank loses a fixed share of its HP to every round that bites, with a chance at the engine; a round that lands in the dirt bursts and kills soldiers close by. The plunging fire drops through tree cover, strikes only near the line it is laid on, and spares your own side — unless you force-attack (then, like a forced bomb, it hits whatever is in the lines, your own units included, and it will lay a barrage on a bare ground point). Sent at an enemy plane it chases it, matches its height, and lays the same barrage through it; on attack-move it takes planes in the air before anything on the ground. It does not attack buildings or drones. On screen each barrage shows as tracer streaks from the wings, with the hits walking up the two lines.

The **BV 222** is a big, slow, unarmed transport flying boat. Its bay takes one load, picked in the unit panel while it is on the pad: **Mines** (a canister that bursts over the point and scatters 14 butterfly mines; they arm after 2 s, go off under enemy feet or tracks — a rifleman dies, a tank loses a little HP and often a track — lie for five minutes, and stay hidden from the enemy until one of his men is close), a **Crate** (a supply crate on a parachute; your units standing at it draw ammo and patch up HP until its 80 points are gone), or **Paratroops** (up to 10 riflemen board it on the hardstand — right-click the parked plane — and **Unload** gets them out again). **Drop here** (force-attack the ground) sends it low and level over the point: the canister or crate goes, or the stick jumps one man after another and drifts down under canopies. Under a canopy a rifleman takes no orders and cannot fire, and rifles and machine guns can reach him. Shot down with the stick aboard, it takes them with it. Empty, it flies home to reload.

The **CIWS** is a small defensive structure: a radar-laid 20mm gatling on a concrete pad. It needs no orders. It swings onto the nearest enemy unit it can hurt, and a plane in the air comes first; it lays on a plane far tighter than small arms do. Tank plate shrugs its rounds off, so it leaves tanks and buildings alone, and tank guns outrange it. Each Titan or Nebelwerfer rocket that flies into its reach draws one short burst per CIWS, with about an even chance of bursting it in the air, where it hurts nothing. The 1,200-round belt does not refill by itself: send a supply truck (right-click the CIWS), and one full truck refills one empty belt. Select a CIWS for three orders. **Rotate** rests the gun on a heading between targets. **Force attack here** (or Ctrl-click) holds it on a ground point or any target, including a building or a tank it would otherwise leave alone, until **Stop** hands it back to its own targeting. Incoming rockets still cut in on a forced aim.

The **RAM** is the CIWS's rocket sibling on the same pad: a radar-laid launcher of short, fast rockets. It picks targets like a CIWS (planes first, then the nearest enemy unit it can hurt; it leaves tanks and buildings alone) and reaches 11 cells, past a CIWS but less than half a Nebelwerfer's reach. It fires a barrage like the Nebelwerfer's, one or two rockets at a time in salvos of eight, but the rockets fly straight and scatter a fraction as wide, so a salvo finds its target. Aimed at a plane, they burst at its height. Each hostile rocket that flies into its reach draws one interceptor off the rack, about one in three bursts it in the air, and each RAM tries each rocket once. It never lays on a high drone. The 32-rocket rack does not refill by itself: send a supply truck. It takes the CIWS's Rotate, Force attack here, and Stop, and the Config panel switches its rockets off to hold fire.

The **Rocketer** (Muster) carries one launcher tube loaded with the Titan's rockets: one straight, fast rocket, then a slow reload off his back (the pack never runs dry). The burst scatters like the pods', tears through soldiers bunched together, dents a tank, and goes up beside a plane or a low drone. A broken arm puts the tube down; he cannot fire while swimming.

The **Pyro** (Muster) carries a flamethrower: two fuel tanks on his back and a lance with a pilot flame. His reach is very short, only a little past a pistol. Each trigger pull throws a one-second burst of burning fuel that arcs onto the ground around the target, splashing the soldiers it lands among; it goes over sandbags and in through the windows of a held house or bunker, burning the men inside without hurting the walls. The tanks hold only three bursts and never refill by themselves (bring a supply truck); dry, he stops going after enemies. Every glob that lands on dry ground sets it burning for about eleven seconds before it dies down, and the burning ground kills unarmored soldiers who stand in it within a couple of seconds, friend or foe. The Cyborg's plating and the Pyro's own suit keep most of it off, soft vehicles scorch, armor plate does not care, and water does not burn. A soldier left idle in the flames walks out; one on Hold stays. When a Pyro is killed there is a small chance (more with full tanks) that the tanks go up in a fireball that kills soldiers next to him and leaves a ring of burning ground. A broken arm puts the lance down; he cannot fire while swimming. He leaves tanks alone on his own.

The **Bunker** (Defences tab) is a low reinforced-concrete pillbox for five infantry: Riflemen, Gunners, Snipers, AT Infantry, Rocketers, Pyros, Medics, and Engineers. Mortarmen, Cyborgs, Drone Ops, and Jump Jets do not fit. It is the best cover on the field: occupants have four times their HP (a house gives three) and the walls stop about two thirds of every hit before it reaches them. Every weapon fires from its slits, the Gunner's MG included; he cannot set his bipod at a house window. It sits low, so unlike a house it adds no sight or reach. A Medic inside slowly heals everyone in it (0.4% of max HP a second) instead of tending one patient by hand; an Engineer inside slowly patches the concrete (1.5 HP a second). More than one of either does not stack. It stays its builder's side's even when empty, so the enemy can shell it or capture it but not walk in, and selling it lets the men out unhurt.

The **Watch Tower** (Defences tab) is a fortified concrete lookout for three infantry, the same troops a Bunker takes. From the cab they see eight tiles farther than on the ground (a house window adds two), and from that height they see over ridges that hide the same ground from a man at its foot. Trees and buildings still block the view. Their weapon reach grows only two tiles, like a house window. Every weapon fires from the slits, the Gunner's MG included. Protection is moderate: occupants have three times their HP, like a house, and the walls stop about two fifths of every hit, where a Bunker stops two thirds. Hiding the garrison shutters the cab and gives up the view. Like the Bunker, it stays its builder's side's even when empty.

An **Engineer** can dig a **Trench** in the field (command bar, beside Sandbags and Obstacle; scroll to turn, drag to dig a line). It is a one-man fighting slit with an earth parapet: anyone the Bunker takes, plus the Mortarman, who can fire his tube from it because it is open to the sky. The cover is moderate: the man inside has double HP (a house gives three times, the Bunker four) and the earth stops about two fifths of every hit. Every weapon works from it, the Gunner's MG included. It adds no sight or reach and blocks no one's path. An empty trench is a hole in the ground, so rounds pass over it and nobody shoots at it until someone is inside. Like the Bunker, it stays its builder's side's.

The **Jump Jet** (Muster) carries an assault rifle (two-round bursts from a thirty-round magazine, a little shorter than the rifle) and a handgun, and wears a jet pack. **Take off** (or **J**) lifts him above the rooftops: he flies straight at his goal over hills, water, walls, and men, faster than he runs. Up there only anti-air weapons reach him: the MG42, the gatlings (Walker, Cyborg, CIWS), the RAM, and the Titan's rockets. Rifles, pistols, tank guns, and mortars cannot. His own bursts come down from above: over sandbags and through the canopy, a crouch or a crawl does not make a man smaller, and a trench's parapet soaks none of it. The pack holds about fourteen seconds of flight. **Land** (or **J** again) sets him down on the nearest open ground, and he lands by himself when the pack runs low. It refills on the ground after a pause of a few seconds. He cannot take off from the water, on a broken leg, or on a nearly empty pack.

The **Drone Op** (Muster) carries no gun. **Launch** puts his one quadcopter up, and it flies as its own unit, straight over everything, but only inside a ring around him (shown while either is selected); if he walks off, the drone is pulled along. **Surveillance** holds it high with wide sight, where only anti-air guns reach it: the MG42 and the gatlings (Walker, Cyborg, CIWS). The RAM's rockets do not. **Search & Destroy** brings it low: it hunts the nearest enemy it can see inside his reach (or the target you give it), dives on it, and bursts. A soldier it lands on dies; on a tank it comes down through the roof. Down low, rifles, machine guns, a tank's coaxial MG, and the Titan's rockets reach it. Tank shells, mortars, and bombs never touch it. The battery lasts about seventy seconds; low on charge the drone flies back by itself (**Recall**, or right-click the operator, does it early) and he stows it to recharge. A drone that is shot down, runs flat, bursts on a target, or loses its operator is gone, and he takes over a minute to build the next one.

The **Nebelwerfer** (Armory) is rocket artillery on a lightly armored truck, and the rockets are its only weapon: twelve tubes on a traversing frame. It must stand still and swing the frame onto the target, then it empties the frame in about a second, one to three rockets at a time (never all twelve together), and reloads for sixteen. The rockets fly like a Titan's — fast, under the same hanging smoke trail, on only a slight arc — and a tank or tree in their path takes the burst. It hits anything your side can see out to 24 cells — the longest reach on the field, far past its own short sight, so it needs a spotter — but it will not fire inside 4 cells. They scatter wide, and each burst is lighter than a Titan rocket: a salvo blankets an area, shreds infantry in the open, and only dents tanks. It never fires at aircraft or drones. The rack holds five salvos (60 rockets); a supply truck refills it, and the Config panel switches the tubes off to hold fire. A CIWS or a RAM can shoot its rockets down.

The **Mammoth** (Armory) is a very slow armored battle platform on two broad tracks: thick plate on every face (F110 / S80 / R60) and a fighting casemate with firing slits on every side. It carries a Bunker's roster — up to five Riflemen, Gunners, Snipers, AT Infantry, Rocketers, Pyros, Medics, or Engineers (no Mortarmen). Select infantry and right-click it (or press the garrison key) to climb in; they chase it if it is driving. Everyone inside fires out of the slits, and the Gunner lays his MG on the ledge, just as in a Bunker; Watch / Hide and Exit work the same way. The hull takes every hit and nothing reaches the soldiers inside, but if it is destroyed, all of them die with it and leave no bodies. It wades through water like the Titan: its own weapon, a small bow machine gun that only swings a little either side of the nose, falls silent there, while the soldiers inside keep firing.

Camera: fixed **2:1 isometric** (RA2 / Sudden Strike 2 feel). **Arrow keys**, **W**/**D**, or middle-mouse drag pan in screen space (**A** and **S** are orders). **H** homes on your HQ. **+** / **−** change game speed (1×–5×, host only). Select the Rig and press **E**, or click it twice, to unpack a Core. Hovering a unit with a ready special swaps the crosshair for a gold pointer. Specials have a cooldown (2s for deploy/pack). Shared orders: **R** rotate (face a point), **G** guard (click and drag to face), **S** stop, **A** attack-move (halt to fire, then keep going), hold **Ctrl** and click to force-attack a point or any unit including friendlies. Guard overwatch: units group there, hold, and fire 360°, but enemies in the cone are engaged first.

Troopers take buildings instead of shooting them: order an attack, stand next to the structure, and hold until the capture bar fills. Drive them off to stop it. Captured buildings change owner. Tigers and StuG IIIs still shell structures. Walker gatlings do not. The StuG has no turret — it hull-steers onto the target.

Units collide and do not pass through each other. Armored vehicles crush opposing infantry if they drive over them. Destroyed Tigers leave a wreck that blocks the tile until you shoot it apart (repair is not in yet). Troopers fire **rifle clips** (eight rounds, then a reload); a **broken arm** swaps to a handgun with its own seven-round magazine. Each trooper is a slightly faster or slower reloader — that pace is rolled when he is trained and never changes. Hits can cripple survivors: infantry may take a **broken arm** (handgun only) or **broken leg** (can only crawl). A **Medic** carries no weapon. If he is not already under orders, he walks to a wounded soldier nearby and closes the wound slowly, and a long kneel sets a broken arm or leg. The bag does not run out. He still bandages someone already in reach while he is holding or marching. A **Cyborg** (Armory) is half soldier, half machine, with 300 HP (a rifleman has 40): one gatling arm at the Walker's cadence, fed from a 600-round drum that only a supply truck refills. He never crouches or goes prone and takes no random limb hits. At 30% health his legs are torn off and he drags himself on one arm, still firing. Even a killing blow only tears the legs off, and for the next 5 seconds nothing can hurt him. A dead cyborg bleeds and his hips spit a few small sparks before going dark. Medics heal him and engineers repair him; back at 60% the legs work again. Motor vehicles risk **broken tracks** on a side hit (20%) or a **dead engine** on a rear hit (40%). Status icons sit next to the unit. Troopers can **Stand**, **Crouch** (C), or **Crawl** (Z). Crouch and crawl shrink their silhouette and tighten their aim; crawl is the stronger of the two. They drop prone on their own when someone is shooting at them. Infantry can **slowly swim** water that vehicles cannot enter, but they cannot fire until they reach shore. The **Titan** is a heavy assault walker with the Tiger's gun on a traversing torso, loaded with AP shot only, and a four-rocket pod on its shoulders. The pods work apart from the main gun, on their own clock and their own target: facing two tanks, the gun takes one and the pods the other. They ripple four rockets one after another, then reload for about ten seconds; the rack holds sixteen and a supply truck refills it, and the Config panel switches the pods off to save them. Rockets fly straight and fast under a thick, hanging smoke trail, with a backblast behind the Titan, scatter wide, and burst like a small mortar bomb: they shred infantry and dent tanks. They also reach planes in the air, bursting beside them; the main gun never does. The Titan wades through water that stops every other vehicle but the Mammoth, with only its torso showing. Its main gun stays silent there, but the rockets still fire. **Deploy** (E) braces it: the outriggers go down, it cannot move, and its hit points grow by three-quarters until it packs up again. The **Apocalypse** is a super-heavy tank: two 105mm guns on one slow turret fire together, two shells a volley, through a Tiger's front plate, under thick plate on every face. A radar-laid 20mm mount on its turret roof lays itself apart from the main guns — planes first, then infantry and light hulls in reach — and tries to burst incoming rockets. Its belt refills only from a supply truck. Tigers carry a mixed rack of about twenty AP / HE / Smoke shells; the StuG III carries only AP and Smoke — pick the load in the right-hand **Config** panel. Smoke shells lay a screen across several tiles that blocks vision for everyone. Force-attacking with Smoke fires one round, then stops. 75mm rounds show a tracer in flight. A Tiger can **open the hatch** (I / Scout) so a crewman looks out: the tank sees as far as infantry, including over a rise, but that head can be shot (about 3× trooper HP). A hull hit buttons him up; remaining HP never comes back, and a dead scout means no more hatch. Mixed selections get type tabs there. Shared orders (Stop, Move attack, Force attack here, Guard, Rotate, Deploy, Harvest, Sell) sit as quick actions on the bottom left.

Hold **Shift** to queue orders. Each Shift-right-click (move, attack, garrison, harvest, repair, supply, board) or Shift-click in Move attack, Force attack here, or Rotate is added behind what the unit is already doing, and the unit carries them out one after another; a group keeps its formation at every stop. Those three modes stay armed while Shift is held, so several points can be clicked in a row. A selected unit shows its queued route as a dashed line: green to walk, red to fight, amber for the rest. Any order given without Shift, or Stop, drops the queue. A point Guard or a Harvest never finishes on its own, so nothing queued after one runs.

**Scrap Yard** is the only map. Dirt lanes run from each start into a cross through the yard. Trees, bushes, and grass tufts each have a few facings and the placer picks one from the tile, so a grove does not all face the same way. The yard has rolling hills and valleys. Units climb slowly and pick cheaper paths around a rise. High ground adds sight and can see the slope below, including each terrace lip. Weapon reach is separate from sight: a rifle stops well inside the fog, a machine gun reaches farther, a scoped rifle farther still, and the Tiger's gun is the longest direct shot — a little past the tank's own eyes, so a spotter is what makes that last band usable. High ground adds a little reach on top of that, not a copy of the wider view. A **Mortarman** kneels, plants the tube, and lobs past every direct-fire gun, over hills and smoke. The round is slow, drifts a little off the aim point, and is devastating to infantry in the open. It will not drop inside a short minimum range. A hit nicks an armored hull, and a tracked tank can lose a track. Accuracy falls off at max range. Ridges block vision past them. Tigers cannot elevate the gun at a steep lip — a hull in a valley or hole cannot hit a target much above it. A hilltop with a clear view fires down; there is no depression limit.

## Prerequisites

- Node.js 22+

## Dev

```bash
cd gridlock && npm install && npm run dev
```

Open two Chrome tabs to http://localhost:5177

**Godot 4 client** (3D placeholders, same hub):

```bash
godot --path godot
```

See [`godot/README.md`](godot/README.md). The Canvas client and the Godot client can both talk to `ws://127.0.0.1:3010/ws`.

## Playtest

Enter a callsign on first launch (saved locally and skipped next time; also in Options).

**Skirmish** is single-player: click Skirmish from the menu and you land in a solo briefing. No Ready switch, no room code, no join.

**Network:**

1. Network → Create game in one tab.
2. Network → Join game by code in the other.
3. Ready both players; host hits Start.
4. You should see a Rig at each spawn. Hover it — the cursor becomes a gold pointer. Click it twice (or select it and press **E**). A progress bar fills for 3 seconds, then a Core appears. Packing is locked for 2 seconds after that.

## Production build

```bash
npm run build && NODE_ENV=production PORT=3010 HOST=127.0.0.1 npm start
```

Ubuntu sketches (systemd + nginx): [`deploy/`](deploy/).
