# Gridlock

Original browser RTS (working title). **Not affiliated with EA.**

## Milestone 2

Menu, lobby, then a match: deploy the **Rig** into a **Core**, build **Power Plant / Smelter / Barracks / Machine Shop / Airfield / Marine Base / CIWS / RAM / Bunker / Tobruk Pit / Heavy Casemate / Watch Tower / Timber Lookout / Fire-Control Tower / Spotlight / MG Nest / Pak 36 / Pak 43 / Flak 37 / Research Facility / Radar Station / Cyborg Central**, earn **scrap** from **Smelters** standing on scrap fields, train **Troopers**, **Gunners**, **Snipers**, **Rocketers**, **Pyros**, **Mortarmen**, **Medics**, **Drone Ops**, **Jump Jets**, **Walkers**, **Cyborgs**, **Cyborg Commanders**, **Titans**, **Mammoths**, **Nebelwerfers**, **Tigers**, **Apocalypses**, **StuG IIIs**, **Jagdtigers**, **Feuerwirbels**, **Stukas**, **Fw 190s**, **BV 222s**, **He 111s**, **Attack Boats**, **Supply Boats**, **Submarines**, **Destroyers**, **Transport LSTs**, and **Battle Ships**, and fight (halt-to-fire). Radar lives in the right command bar, and stays dark until a **Radar Station** stands; its dish also hears enemy aircraft far past anyone's eyes and blinks them on the panel only. Tigers, Apocalypses, Jagdtigers, Feuerwirbels, Cyborg Commanders, Titans, Mammoths, Nebelwerfers, Drone Ops, and Jump Jets also need a standing **Research Facility**, and Cyborgs and Cyborg Commanders a standing **Cyborg Central**; lose one and those cameos lock again (jobs already queued keep going).

Scrap is the only currency. A **Smelter** has to stand on a scrap field, with at least half its footprint on scrap, and from then on it pours scrap every second for as long as it stands; the field never runs out, every Smelter adds its own share, and short power slows the pour the way it slows production. The yard places a Smelter within build range of your base. A selected **engineer** can also raise one on any scrap field he can walk to (the Smelter button on his panel, then click the field), which is how distant scrap is claimed and how your build range reaches out to it. Only base buildings carry build range: the Defences tab reaches a little farther than base buildings do, so guns, garrisons, and lines can ring a base or a field Smelter, but a tower or a wall never extends the yard. A Smelter that falls stops paying; capture or rebuild to get the field back. There is no harvester unit for now.

The **Airfield** trains **Stuka** dive bombers, **Fw 190** fighters, **BV 222** transports, and **He 111** torpedo bombers and parks up to four in the sandbagged hardstands beside its long strip. A plane taxis out onto the strip and takes off on any order; coming home it lands from the far end, rolls out, and taxis back into its revetment. In the air it flies straight over hills, trees, and water, and circles the point it was sent to. On attack it dives, drops one SC 250 (a direct hit takes half a tank through the roof; a near miss throws tracks and dents the hull; infantry under the blast die, and the rim only wounds), strafes soft targets with its wing MGs, then climbs out and comes round again. It flies home by itself when the bomb and belts are spent or the fuel runs low (**Return**, or right-click your Airfield, sends it early), then refuels, rearms, and patches up on the pad. **Guard** (G) sends it to an area: it fights there, and when the ammo is gone it lands, rearms to a full load, and flies back to that area until given another order. With no Airfield left it circles until the tank is dry and goes down. A plane has no tracks to lose. A hit that wrecks the engine brings it down at once. Only small arms reach a plane in the air from the ground — rifles, machine guns, the PTRD, and the Walker — at a wide spread. Tank guns and mortars cannot. A parked plane is an ordinary ground target.

The **Fw 190** carries no bomb: two 30 mm cannon, one under each wing, and three barrages a sortie. It is faster and turns tighter than the Stuka. On a ground target it dives, and when the target is close and on the nose it looses one barrage: both wings at once, two straight parallel lines of rounds laid along the bearing to the target, running from short of it to past it. Then it flies out, comes round, and fires the next; after the third it goes home to rearm. On guard it comes back to that area with all three barrages. Fired from above, the rounds come down on a hull's roof (about a third of its side plate), so even the heaviest tank loses a fixed share of its HP to every round that bites, with a chance at the engine; a round that lands in the dirt bursts and kills soldiers close by. The plunging fire drops through tree cover, strikes only near the line it is laid on, and spares your own side — unless you force-attack (then, like a forced bomb, it hits whatever is in the lines, your own units included, and it will lay a barrage on a bare ground point). Sent at an enemy plane it chases it, matches its height, and lays the same barrage through it; on attack-move it takes planes in the air before anything on the ground. It does not attack buildings or drones. On screen each barrage shows as tracer streaks from the wings, with the hits walking up the two lines.

The **BV 222** is a big, slow, unarmed transport flying boat. Its bay takes one load, picked in the unit panel while it is on the pad: **Mines** (a canister that bursts over the point and scatters 14 mines; they arm after 2 s, go off under any feet or tracks, friend or foe — a rifleman dies, a tank loses a little HP and often a track — and lie for five minutes. The enemy never sees them. You and your allies see a small dot that blinks a faint red light. A selected supply truck can right-click one of those to disable it; after a short wait the mine is gone and the scrap is added), a **Crate** (a supply crate on a parachute; your units standing at it draw ammo and patch up HP until its 80 points are gone), or **Paratroops** (up to 10 ground units. Selected infantry can right-click the parked plane from any load; climbing in selects Paratroops, and no other load can be chosen while anyone is aboard. Vehicles board once Paratroops is already selected. **Unload** gets them out on the pad). **Drop here** (force-attack the ground, or hold Ctrl and click) sends it low and level over the point for every load: the canister or crate goes, or the stick jumps one after another and drifts down under canopies. Under a canopy a jumper takes no orders and cannot fire, and rifles, machine guns, and anti-aircraft guns (the CIWS, the RAM, the Walker) can reach them. While anyone is aboard, their hit-point bars sit beside the plane. Shot down in the air, or with its engine wrecked there, everyone still aboard bails out under canopies and then it falls trailing smoke and crashes as a wreck. On the pad the same hit puts them on the grass and the plane is gone. Empty, it flies home to reload.

The **He 111** is a twin-engined torpedo bomber with no guns and no bomb: one torpedo under the belly, the very one a Submarine fires (same warhead, speed, spread, and body any gun can shoot apart). It goes only after what is in the water — boats, a submarine surfaced or down, swimmers, a Marine Base — and on attack-move, guard, or patrol it passes over everything ashore. On the way in it comes down low over the water, lines up, and lets go once the target is a Submarine's torpedo reach off the nose and there is water under the plane; over land it holds the torpedo and comes round again. The torpedo always runs a torpedo's full length, even on a force attack laid on a nearer point, and strikes the first thing afloat across its path, friend or foe, surfaced or submerged; it still dies where the water ends. One torpedo a sortie, then it flies home and hangs another on the pad. Low on its run, it is in easy reach of rifles and anti-aircraft guns.

The **CIWS** is a small defensive structure: a radar-laid 20mm gatling on a concrete pad. It needs no orders. It swings onto the nearest enemy unit it can hurt, and a plane or a paratrooper under a canopy comes first; it lays on a plane far tighter than small arms do. Tank plate shrugs its rounds off, so it leaves tanks and buildings alone. A Walker or a truck sometimes takes a round. Tank guns outrange it. Each Titan or Nebelwerfer rocket that flies into its reach draws one short burst per CIWS, with about an even chance of bursting it in the air, where it hurts nothing. The 1,200-round belt does not refill by itself: send a supply truck (right-click the CIWS), and one full truck refills one empty belt. Select a CIWS for three orders. **Rotate** rests the gun on a heading between targets. **Force attack here** (or Ctrl-click) holds it on a ground point or any target, including a building or a tank it would otherwise leave alone, until **Stop** hands it back to its own targeting. Incoming rockets still cut in on a forced aim.

The **RAM** is the CIWS's rocket sibling on the same pad: a radar-laid launcher of short, fast rockets. It picks targets like a CIWS (a plane or a paratrooper under a canopy first, then the nearest enemy unit it can hurt; it leaves tanks and buildings alone) and reaches 11 cells, past a CIWS but less than half a Nebelwerfer's reach. It fires a barrage like the Nebelwerfer's, one or two rockets at a time in salvos of eight, but the rockets fly straight and scatter a fraction as wide, and tighter still when the target is inside that reach, so a salvo finds its target. Aimed at a plane, they burst at its height. Each hostile rocket that flies into its reach draws one interceptor off the rack, about one in three bursts it in the air, and each RAM tries each rocket once. It never lays on a high drone. The 32-rocket rack does not refill by itself: send a supply truck. It takes the CIWS's Rotate, Force attack here (or Ctrl-click), and Stop, and the Config panel switches its rockets off to hold fire.

The **Marine Base** is a floating pier and slipway that can only be built on water: every tile under it must be open water (the ghost stays red otherwise). It trains the navy, which has its own **Sea** tab, and its boats launch onto the water beside it. Boats move on water tiles only and never come ashore; a move order onto land stops at the waterline. They fire from the water, cast no shadow on it, and have no tracks to throw (a rear hit can still kill the engine). Sunk, every ship settles where it went down and leaves a hulk on the bottom, in plain sight even if it was a diving submarine. Like a tank wreck it blocks the water there until it is force-attacked and shot apart (torpedoes and blasts tear at it too). Engineers cannot reach it to cut it up. They cannot board a BV 222. The **Attack Boat** is a fast, thin-skinned motor gunboat with a 20mm cannon that fires on boats and on anything within reach of the bank, and unlike a tank gun it lays up a raised shore. The **Submarine** is slower and a fifth bigger. It leaves the slip surfaced; **Dive** and **Surface** in the command bar set its depth. Submerged, the enemy sees it only within four cells of one of their units or buildings, or for four seconds after it fires (its owner sees it faint under the surface), and its torpedoes find only another submarine that is down too: it must surface to strike anything else, and ordered to attack or force-attack a surface target it closes in submerged and surfaces once in range. It holds 150 seconds of air below; when that runs out it surfaces and stays up until its air is back. Its bow tubes fire slow torpedoes that run in plain sight at the waterline as their own targetable body — any gun can shoot one apart before it arrives — and only strike what floats or stands in the water — boats, swimmers, a wading walker, a Marine Base. A torpedo dies where the water ends, so a spit of land shields what lies behind it. Turn the bow to aim. Boats, like tanks, swing onto their course before they make way. The **Battle Ship** (after the Iowa class) carries two triple 16-inch turrets on the foredeck. Every barrel loads, fires, and holds its shells on its own: a turret on target lets its loaded barrels go one by one in a random order, a short gap apart. The shell is the field gun's on a low, fast arc, so it lands almost at once but reaches less far than Artillery, and the forward turrets cannot fire astern through the superstructure (the hull comes round instead). Two radar-laid 20mm CIWS mounts, on the superstructure and the stern, lay themselves like the Apocalypse's roof mount, each on its own belt. Its belt armor turns most tank fire; torpedoes and heavy shells are what sink it. Beside a friendly Marine Base its barrels and belts slowly fill again. The **Supply Boat** is the supply truck's work on the water: an unarmed cargo launch that tops up a Battle Ship's barrels and CIWS belts from its cargo, goes on its own to allied ships nearby that are short, scrounges its cargo back slowly, and refills fast beside a Marine Base (right-click the Marine Base). It serves only what floats, and the supply truck only what stands ashore. It carries no passengers, tows nothing, and does not lift mines. The **Destroyer** has a fast twin 40mm on the foredeck that reaches only seven cells. Its hull sonar hears every enemy submarine within sixteen cells, submerged or surfaced. Each new contact is called out ("Sonar contact") and pings on the map and the radar panel over the fog, but hearing is not seeing: a gun still lays only on a submarine that is in sight. On a contact the ship's **ASW helicopter** takes off by itself. Nobody gives it orders. It flies at the heard position (the sonar keeps the plot fresh while it still hears the boat) and, from up to four cells out with water under it, drops three torpedoes in a fan. Each is the He 111's torpedo: it runs a torpedo's full length and finds a submarine down or up. The helicopter then flies home, lands on the fantail, and loads again for 25 seconds. Rifles, machine guns, and anti-air reach it in the air; shot down, the ship gets a new one after 90 seconds, and it goes down with its ship. **Lay Mine** in the command bar puts one of the Destroyer's three horned contact mines over the stern, one at a time. It lives four seconds later and goes off under any hull, swimmer, or submarine (submerged too) that meets it, friend or foe — though the ship that laid it is spared until it has steamed clear. A plane or a running torpedo passes over it. Only you and your allies see your mines. Beside a Marine Base the rail fills again, one mine every ten seconds. The **Transport LST** is a slow, heavily plated landing ship with a tank deck of 40 slots: a soldier takes one, a Cyborg two, a Walker three, a field gun or supply truck four, a StuG or Feuerwirbel six, a Tiger eight, a Jagdtiger or Titan ten, an Apocalypse twelve, and a Mammoth sixteen. Right-click it with infantry or vehicles selected and they board up the bow ramp once its bow is on the beach; Unload puts everyone down the ramp onto dry ground, and nothing lands while the bow is out at sea. The first two soldiers aboard who can shoot man its two deck machine guns from shielded tubs, with Bunker-grade protection; nobody else aboard fires, and if the ship is sunk everyone aboard goes down with it.

The **Rocketer** (Barracks) carries one launcher tube loaded with the Titan's rockets: one straight, fast rocket, then a slow reload off his back (the pack never runs dry). The burst scatters like the pods' — wide at full reach, tighter as the target closes — tears through soldiers bunched together, dents a tank, and a side or rear hit usually breaks its tracks. It goes up beside a plane or a low drone. Config also loads one **high-penetration** missile: half again as fast, still accurate at full reach, and a direct hit wrecks armor. He carries only one. Fitting it takes a few seconds unless it is already on the tube, he fires it only when you order the shot, and a supply truck has to bring another. A CIWS or a RAM has to connect several times to bring that missile down. A broken arm puts the tube down; he cannot fire while swimming.

The **Pyro** (Barracks) carries a flamethrower: two fuel tanks on his back and a lance with a pilot flame. His reach is very short, only a little past a pistol. Each trigger pull throws a short burst of burning fuel. The jet burns every soldier it passes, friend or foe, the way the Cyborg Commander's beam does, and every tree on that line. A building or a concrete line stops it. It leaves the ground burning from a little past him out to the target, and the globs still splash where they land. It goes over sandbags and in through the windows of a held house or bunker, burning the men inside without hurting the walls. The tanks hold four bursts and a short one and never refill by themselves (bring a supply truck); dry, he stops going after enemies. Every glob that lands on dry ground sets it burning for about eleven seconds before it dies down, and the burning ground kills unarmored soldiers who stand in it within a couple of seconds, friend or foe. The Cyborg's plating and the Pyro's own suit keep most of it off, soft vehicles scorch, armor plate does not care, and water does not burn. A soldier left idle in the flames walks out; one on Hold stays. When a Pyro is killed there is a small chance (more with full tanks) that the tanks go up in a fireball that kills soldiers next to him and leaves a ring of burning ground. A broken arm puts the lance down; he cannot fire while swimming. He leaves tanks alone on his own.

A **Bunker**, **Watch Tower**, **Airfield**, **CIWS**, **RAM**, and every new fort and gun below turns before you place it, like a wall: scroll the wheel and it swings 15° a notch (a tag by the cursor says so and shows the heading), and its footprint on the grid turns with it. A turned Airfield lays its strip and hardstands the new way, and its planes take off and land along it. A turned Watch Tower starts its searchlight along its new front.

The **Bunker** (Defences tab) is a low reinforced-concrete pillbox for five infantry: Riflemen, Gunners, Snipers, AT Infantry, Rocketers, Pyros, Medics, and Engineers. Mortarmen, Cyborgs, Cyborg Commanders, Drone Ops, and Jump Jets do not fit. It is the best cover on the field: occupants have four times their HP (a house gives three) and the walls stop about two thirds of every hit before it reaches them. Every weapon fires from its slits, the Gunner's MG included; he cannot set his bipod at a house window. It sits low, so unlike a house it adds no sight or reach. A Medic inside slowly heals everyone in it (0.4% of max HP a second) instead of tending one patient by hand; an Engineer inside slowly patches the concrete (1.5 HP a second). More than one of either does not stack. It stays its builder's side's even when empty, so the enemy can shell it or capture it but not walk in, and selling it lets the men out unhurt.

The **Watch Tower** (Defences tab) is a fortified concrete lookout for three infantry, the same troops a Bunker takes. From the cab they see eight tiles farther than on the ground (a house window adds two), and from that height they see over ridges that hide the same ground from a man at its foot. Trees and buildings still block the view. Their weapon reach grows only two tiles, like a house window. Every weapon fires from the slits, the Gunner's MG included. Protection is moderate: occupants have three times their HP, like a house, and the walls stop about two fifths of every hit, where a Bunker stops two thirds. Hiding the garrison shutters the cab and gives up the view. Like the Bunker, it stays its builder's side's even when empty. At night the searchlight on its roof lights a cone of ground, and the lamp turns with the beam. A bullet can smash the lamps on a hull or on this tower — a sniper's shot does it more often — and every lamp on that body goes dark together. An engineer fits a new one.

More forts, all on the Defences tab and in the Map Builder, in a WW2 German look. The **Tobruk Pit** is a concrete ring-stand sunk almost flush with the ground for two men — a Bunker's troops or a Mortarman, who works his tube from the open hatch — cheap, quick, and hard to hit, but a mortar bomb that drops in finds them. The **Heavy Casemate** is a 3×2 Atlantic-wall block for eight: twice a Bunker's HP, even less of each hit reaches the men, a steel cupola adds three tiles of sight, and a Medic or Engineer inside works faster. The **Timber Lookout** is a log tower for two with the farthest eyes on the field (eleven tiles over the ground) and three tiles of reach, but the planks stop very little. The **Fire-Control Tower** is a 2×2 flak-tower block for six, walls almost as good as a Bunker's, nine tiles of sight and four of reach, and the Watch Tower's spotlight. The **Spotlight** (150 scrap) is that same searchlight on a steel pole over a ring of sandbags, raised with one rifleman already at it. Turn it before placing to set where the lamp first looks; afterwards Rotate swings it and Patrol sweeps it between spots, as on the tower. The lamp burns only with someone at it: the man takes three quarters of each hit on the post, and if he dies the lamp goes dark until another soldier from a Bunker's list garrisons it. A bullet can smash the lamp and leave him standing, and an engineer fits a new one; fell the pole and both are gone. Enemy infantry cannot capture it.

The **crewed guns** are emplacements that go up with their crew already at them: the **MG Nest** (an MG42 on its tripod inside a ring of decorative sandbags, one man), the **Pak 36** (light 37mm anti-tank gun, one man), the **Pak 43** (the 88mm on its cross platform, two men), and the **Flak 37** (37mm anti-aircraft gun, two men). The crew are Riflemen garrisoned in the gun. The gun fires only while someone living is at it, and the crew's own weapons stay slung. Rifle and MG fire on a gun finds the crew, not the steel; when the last man falls it goes silent, enemies stop wasting rounds on it, and any infantry a Bunker takes can walk in to man it again (short-handed, a two-man gun fires and reloads at half pace). Hiding the crew holds its fire. The MG Nest sweeps 60° either side of the way it was turned and the Pak 36 only 30°; what stands behind that arc is safe, and the placement ghost and a selected gun show the arc on the ground. The Pak 43 turns all the way round but slowly, and reaches farther than any other direct-fire gun. Both Paks fire armor-piercing shells like a StuG's and pick tanks first. The Flak 37 lays only on what flies (planes, Jump Jets, drones, men under canopies): it leads a plane on its heading and fires time-fused shells that burst at its height in a black cloud, hurting everything flying inside the burst, so one shell can catch two planes; the fuse and the aim scatter, so it is only moderately accurate. Force attack on the ground puts a barrage up over that point. The MG Nest reaches aircraft too. Every gun has a finite store — 30 shells for the Pak 36, 16 for the Pak 43, 64 for the Flak, 1,000 rounds for the MG Nest — shown as a yellow bar under its health; none of it refills by itself, so right-click the gun with a supply truck. The Paks and the Flak throw a field gun's blast cloud round the pit when they fire (the Pak 43's far bigger, and its shells strike bigger); the MG Nest flashes at the muzzle like a gatling. None of them move. A map's guns stand with neutral crews; once a crew falls, the first soldier to man the gun takes it for his side.

An **Engineer** can dig a **Trench** in the field (command bar, beside Sandbags and Obstacle; scroll to turn, drag to dig a line). It is a one-man fighting slit with an earth parapet: anyone the Bunker takes, plus the Mortarman, who can fire his tube from it because it is open to the sky. The cover is moderate: the man inside has double HP (a house gives three times, the Bunker four) and the earth stops about two fifths of every hit. Every weapon works from it, the Gunner's MG included. It adds no sight or reach and blocks no one's path. An empty trench is a hole in the ground, so rounds pass over it and nobody shoots at it until someone is inside. Like the Bunker, it stays its builder's side's.

**Sandbags** and a **Wall** are on the Defences tab as well as the engineer's command bar. From the tab you lay the line first, and only next to your own buildings. The yard then builds it on a lane of its own, so a line goes up at the same time as a base structure and a gun or bunker; one line at a time. A longer line takes longer and costs the same again for each section. An engineer still places either anywhere on the map.

Every field line is laid the same way. Scroll to turn the piece, then press and drag the first run. Release, and the line waits at the end of that run: move the cursor and click to add another leg, as many as you like, round any corner up to a hairpin; there is no limit on how far a line reaches. Right-click takes the last leg back, Esc drops the drawing. The **Confirm placement** button on the command bar (or Enter) sends the whole line as one order. Where a line turns, the next leg starts in the mitre of the corner, so sandbags, walls, and Large walls join up as one continuous work with no gap on the outer corner and nothing poking into the inner one; the concrete runs its outer face round the corner, and sandbags are piled into the angle. All three cast a shadow, and the concrete is weathered: grime at the foot, run-off streaks, chipped edges.

The **Large wall** (type id `greatwall`) is hidden for now: it stays in the sim but is off the Defences tab and the engineer's command bar (`HIDDEN_FIELD_TYPES` in the catalog). It is a tall concrete wall section with firing slits down both faces, the same length as the ordinary wall and half as thick again, laid the same way and joined the same way. Nothing walks through it and no direct fire crosses it: a bullet stops on the concrete, a tank shell or a rocket chips it, and an engineer repairs it. Two of the infantry a Bunker takes garrison each section and fire every weapon from the slits, with triple health; the concrete passes half of each hit that lands on a manned section through to them, and they see and reach two cells farther than on the ground. A manned section's slits glow in the holder's colour. It cannot be captured; it has to be shot apart.

An **Engineer** raises a **Wall** from the command bar. Scroll to turn it before you set the start, then drag to the end and the sections sit end to end. On the command bar he builds the whole line as one job, and it takes longer the more sections there are. When he finishes, the concrete appears at once, with barbed wire along the top. From the Defences tab the line is sited first and appears when the yard finishes that same timed build. While it stands, nothing walks through it. Tank shells and rockets break it; he can repair what is still standing. A unit beside the wall has extra health and takes less from fire along the ground. A mortar, a bomb, or a shot from the air ignores that cover. On a slope the tops of a line stay in one level: when the line is raised, every section takes the highest ground under any of them and under any standing wall it joins, and the joined wall rises to match; that crest only ever rises. A section on lower ground stretches down to the dirt, a neighbour falling never lowers the rest, and damage shows as cracks, never as a lower slab. The concrete is a solid slab: the edge lines are not drawn, and where two sections meet the joint is closed.

**Bridges** are hidden for now: they stay in the sim but are off the engineer's command bar (`BRIDGES_HIDDEN` in the catalog). An **Engineer** bridges small water: the **Wooden bridge** (one tank wide) or the **Concrete bridge** (two tanks abreast). Press on one shore and drag (or click) to the other: the deck snaps to the crossing and rests on dry land at each end, up to ten cells long, and the ghost shows its price or why it will not fit. The price and the work grow with the length. The nearest selected engineer walks to the near end, pays when he starts, and the whole bridge appears at once when he finishes. A bridge belongs to no one: anyone crosses it, tanks drive over the water, infantry walk instead of swimming, and boats cannot pass under. Nothing fires on a bridge by itself and a right-click on it is a move; only a **force-attack** aims at it, and only shells, rockets, mortar and field-gun rounds and bombs hurt it (a wooden one falls to a few HE shells, a concrete one takes a long shelling). When it falls, every vehicle on the deck goes down with it and the men on it are left swimming. The wreckage stays on both banks and cannot be destroyed; any engineer can right-click it to rebuild it, or patch a damaged deck.

A **Gate** is built from the Defences tab: point it at two of your own Wall sections side by side, and when the yard finishes it (40 scrap) the two sections become one wide gate, keeping their share of health and the line's height. It is two concrete posts with a small lamp on each, and a striped boom between them, like a car-park barrier. The boom lifts while one of your side's ground units is near and drops when they have gone; units of the other side stop at it as at the wall. Rounds fly through the gap while the boom is up and strike it while it is down. A gate gives no cover. Lock gate drops the boom and keeps it down for everyone, with a padlock drawn over the gate; Unlock lets it lift again. A gate has the hit points of the two sections it replaced and falls like a wall.

The **Jump Jet** (Barracks) carries an assault rifle (two-round bursts from a thirty-round magazine, a little shorter than the rifle) and a handgun, and wears a jet pack. **Take off** (or **J**) lifts him above the rooftops: he flies straight at his goal over hills, water, walls, and men, faster than he runs. Up there only anti-air weapons reach him: the MG42, the gatlings (Walker, Cyborg, CIWS), the RAM, and the Titan's rockets. Rifles, pistols, tank guns, and mortars cannot. His own bursts come down from above: over sandbags and through the canopy, a crouch or a crawl does not make a man smaller, and a trench's parapet soaks none of it. The pack holds about fourteen seconds of flight. **Land** (or **J** again) sets him down on the nearest open ground, and he lands by himself when the pack runs low. It refills on the ground after a pause of a few seconds. He cannot take off from the water, on a broken leg, or on a nearly empty pack.

The **Drone Op** (Barracks) carries no gun. **Launch** puts his one quadcopter up, and it flies as its own unit, straight over everything, but only inside a ring around him (shown while either is selected); if he walks off, the drone is pulled along. **Surveillance** holds it high with wide sight, where only anti-air guns reach it: the MG42 and the gatlings (Walker, Cyborg, CIWS). The RAM's rockets do not. **Search & Destroy** brings it low: it hunts the nearest enemy it can see inside his reach (or the target you give it), dives on it, and bursts. A soldier it lands on dies; on a tank it comes down through the roof. Down low, rifles, machine guns, a tank's coaxial MG, and the Titan's rockets reach it. Tank shells, mortars, and bombs never touch it. The battery lasts about seventy seconds; low on charge the drone flies back by itself (**Recall**, or right-click the operator, does it early) and he stows it to recharge. A drone that is shot down, runs flat, bursts on a target, or loses its operator is gone, and he takes over a minute to build the next one.

The **Nebelwerfer** (Machine Shop) is rocket artillery on a lightly armored truck, and the rockets are its only weapon: twelve tubes on a traversing frame. It must stand still and swing the frame onto the target, then it empties the frame in about a second, one to three rockets at a time (never all twelve together), and reloads for sixteen. The rockets fly like a Titan's — fast, under the same hanging smoke trail, on only a slight arc — and a tank or tree in their path takes the burst. It hits anything your side can see out to 24 cells — the longest reach on the field, far past its own short sight, so it needs a spotter — but it will not fire inside 4 cells. At full reach they scatter wide, and they draw in as the target sits closer inside that reach. Each burst is lighter than a Titan rocket: a salvo blankets an area and shreds infantry in the open. Armor only dents, but a hit on a tracked hull from the side or the rear usually throws a track. It never fires at aircraft or drones. The rack holds five salvos (60 rockets); a supply truck refills it, and the Config panel switches the tubes off to hold fire. A CIWS or a RAM can shoot its rockets down.

The **Mammoth** (Machine Shop) is a very slow armored battle platform on four legs: thick plate on every face (F110 / S80 / R60), a cab out over the bow, and side pods with firing slits down both flanks. It carries a Bunker's roster — up to five Riflemen, Gunners, Snipers, AT Infantry, Rocketers, Pyros, Medics, or Engineers (no Mortarmen). Select infantry and right-click it (or press the garrison key) to climb in; they chase it if it is driving. Everyone inside fires out of the slits, and the Gunner lays his MG on the ledge, just as in a Bunker; Watch / Hide and Exit work the same way. The hull takes every hit and nothing reaches the soldiers inside, but if it is destroyed, all of them die with it and leave no bodies. Force attack on the hull aims every soldier inside who can reach that point; they stay aboard. In water it is thirty percent slower and wades waist deep, with only the body above the surface. Its own weapon, a twin machine gun under the cab that only swings a little either side of the nose, falls silent there, while the soldiers inside keep firing. Nothing throws a track. A hit in the rear can still wreck the engine and stop it. At night a lamp on the nose and one on each flank light the ground out to its daylight sight, and the flank lamps drift slowly through a small arc. A bullet can put all three out at once, and a sniper's shot does it more often.

Camera: fixed **2:1 isometric** (RA2 / Sudden Strike 2 feel). **Arrow keys**, **W**/**D**, or middle-mouse drag pan in screen space (**A** and **S** are orders). **H** homes on your HQ. **+** / **−** change game speed (1×–5×, host only). Select the Rig and press **E**, or click it twice, to unpack a Core. Hovering a unit with a ready special swaps the crosshair for a gold pointer. Specials have a cooldown (2s for deploy/pack). Shared orders: **R** rotate (face a point), **G** guard (click and drag to face), **S** stop, **A** attack-move (halt to fire, then keep going), **Y** patrol (left-click each point, right-click to finish; click a point already placed to close a loop and circle it, otherwise they walk the points and back; every unit that can fight breaks off when an enemy comes into range of that path, then resumes; a Watch Tower or a Fire-Control Tower takes the same points and turns its spotlight the same way), hold **Ctrl** and click to force-attack a point or any unit including friendlies. Hold the right button on open ground and a **Rotate** mark appears at the spot: drag to aim, release, and the unit walks there and then turns to that heading. A quick right-click still only moves. On a Mammoth, or on a bunker, tower, house, or trench you occupy, that order also aims every soldier inside who can fire from it and can reach the point. They stay inside. Stop on the host drops the aim. Guard overwatch: units group there, hold, and fire 360°, but enemies in the cone are engaged first.

Troopers take buildings instead of shooting them: order an attack, stand next to the structure, and hold until the capture bar fills. Drive them off to stop it. Captured buildings change owner. Tigers and StuG IIIs still shell structures. Walker gatlings do not. A round sometimes bites a Walker or a truck; tank plate turns it. At a fifth of his health a Walker charges the nearest enemy he can see and detonates, unless Self destroy is off in Config. That remainder swells to five times the hit points, still a fifth of his bar, and he runs about 40% faster with a short trail of dark smoke. The blast nicks a tank and hits everything else harder, and he leaves no wreck. Shot apart any other way, he still leaves one. The StuG has no turret — it hull-steers onto the target. Neither does the **Jagdtiger** (Machine Shop), a heavy tank destroyer: its 128mm sits in a fixed casemate that swings only ten degrees either side of the nose, on a slow hull that turns slower still. It has the thickest front plate on the field (F150 / S50 / R30) and the longest reach of any tank gun, and its AP goes through any front plate and usually kills a Tiger in one hit. It reloads slowly and carries only AP and HE, with no HEAT or Smoke. The **Feuerwirbel** (Machine Shop, Research Facility) is a flame tank for soldiers and aircraft: two gatlings side by side on the fastest turret on the field look for a plane, a Jump Jet aloft, or a drone first, then hose infantry. They sometimes bite a Walker or a truck, leave tank plate and buildings alone unless ordered, and overheat after about two seconds on the trigger. A flame projector fixed in the bow fires on its own clock at soldiers, soft vehicles, and manned houses inside a short reach, but only within a few degrees of the nose; stopped, the hull turns onto a target close enough to burn, and a force-attack on the ground burns that spot once the nose bears. The jet holds while a friendly soldier stands in it. The gatling belt and ten bursts of fuel refill only from a supply truck.

Units collide and do not pass through each other. Armored vehicles crush opposing infantry if they drive over them. Destroyed Tigers leave a wreck that blocks the tile until you shoot it apart (repair is not in yet). Troopers fire **rifle clips** (eight rounds, then a reload); a **broken arm** swaps to a handgun with its own seven-round magazine. Each trooper is a slightly faster or slower reloader — that pace is rolled when he is trained and never changes. Hits can cripple survivors: infantry may take a **broken arm** (handgun only) or **broken leg** (can only crawl). A **Medic** carries no weapon. If he is not already under orders, he walks to a wounded soldier nearby and closes the wound slowly, and a long kneel sets a broken arm or leg. The bag does not run out. He still bandages someone already in reach while he is holding or marching. A **Cyborg** (Machine Shop) is half soldier, half machine, with 300 HP (a rifleman has 40): one gatling arm at the Walker's cadence, fed from a 600-round drum that only a supply truck refills. A round sometimes bites a Walker or a truck. He never crouches or goes prone and takes no random limb hits. At 30% health his legs are torn off and he drags himself on one arm, still firing. Even a killing blow only tears the legs off, and for the next 5 seconds nothing can hurt him. A dead cyborg bleeds and his hips spit a few small sparks before going dark. Medics heal him and engineers repair him; back at 60% the legs work again. Cyborgs run on a **link**: your own standing **Cyborg Central** while your power is not short, or your own living Cyborg Commander. With neither, four seconds later (the power readout counts it down) every Cyborg of yours on the field shuts down: he goes dark where he stands, belongs to no one, takes no orders, and fires at nothing, and nobody fires at him unless force-attacking. One aboard a transport waits until he steps off. A living **Cyborg Commander** takes over any shut-down Cyborg within eight cells, yours or the enemy's, one at a time, three seconds each, with no order needed: a cyan uplink runs to him and a ring closes as it works; it starts over if the Commander leaves or falls. The **Cyborg Commander** (Machine Shop, 5000 scrap, needs a Research Facility and a Cyborg Central) is the same frame with the same crawl rule, plus a pale-blue **force field** (200 points, its own bar above his health) that takes every hit before his plating does; after 7 seconds without a hit it recharges, 12 once it was knocked down. His arm is a **cutting laser** with a longer reach than the Cyborg's gatling that recharges for about 4 seconds between shots and never needs a truck. On soldiers (or a point on the ground) it always cuts out to full reach and sweeps a solid red beam across the aim, 14 degrees either side, in about half a second: every soldier the beam passes burns down where he stands, friend or foe (a Cyborg's plating takes a heavy cut instead), and the tip leaves a thin line of small fires along the arc at full reach. Every tree the beam crosses burns down, sweep or single beam; trees do not stop it, buildings and concrete do. On a hull, a building, or a wreck it is one straight beam that cuts any plate from any face for a moderate 30 HP, and burns any soldier, his own included, standing between him and the target. Motor vehicles risk **broken tracks** on a side hit (20%) or a **dead engine** on a rear hit (40%). Status icons sit next to the unit. Troopers can **Stand**, **Crouch** (C), or **Crawl** (Z). Crouch and crawl shrink their silhouette and tighten their aim; crawl is the stronger of the two. They drop prone on their own when someone is shooting at them. Infantry can **slowly swim** water that vehicles cannot enter, but they cannot fire until they reach shore. A player has at most one **Titan** and one **Cyborg Commander** at a time: while one stands, or one is already in a production queue (any Machine Shop), another cannot be ordered; once it is destroyed the cameo opens again. The **Titan** is a heavy assault walker with the Tiger's gun on a traversing torso, loaded with AP shot only, and a four-rocket pod on its shoulders. The pods work apart from the main gun, on their own clock and their own target: facing two tanks, the gun takes one and the pods the other. They ripple four rockets one after another, then reload for about ten seconds; the rack holds sixteen and a supply truck refills it, and the Config panel switches the pods off to save them. Rockets fly straight and fast under a thick, hanging smoke trail, with a backblast behind the Titan. At full reach they scatter wide, and they draw in as the target closes inside that reach. They burst like a small mortar bomb: they shred infantry and dent tanks, and a rocket that hits a tracked hull from the side or the rear usually throws a track. They also reach planes in the air, bursting beside them; the main gun never does. The Titan wades through water that stops every other vehicle but the Mammoth, with only its torso showing. Its main gun stays silent there, but the rockets still fire. **Deploy** (E) braces it: the outriggers go down, it cannot move, and its hit points grow by three-quarters until it packs up again. **Take off** (or **J**) fires its leg jets for a short hop of about six seconds straight over anything; up there the main gun is stowed and only the pods fire, only anti-air weapons reach it, and the burners take about half a minute to recover after it sets down. It costs 8000 scrap, and its reactor makes it a bomb: destroyed, it goes up in a small nuclear blast — a white flash, a shockwave, and a mushroom cloud — that wrecks everything within about four and a half cells, friend or foe, and leaves the ground burning and scorched. Shot down in the air, it first drops straight down and goes up on the ground. The **Apocalypse** is a super-heavy tank: two 105mm guns on one slow turret fire one after the other, a short gap between the barrels and then a long reload, through a Tiger's front plate, under thick plate on every face. A small radar-laid 20mm CIWS on its turret roof lays itself apart from the main guns — incoming missiles first, and it bursts most of them, then planes, infantry, and sometimes a Walker or a truck. Its belt refills only from a supply truck. Tigers carry a rack of eighteen AP and HE shells; the StuG III carries only AP — pick the load in the right-hand **Config** panel. A tank's HE shell bursts where it first stops — it does not skip off armor — in a fireball as big as a tank going up, and the ground around it keeps burning like the Pyro's fuel, deadly to any soldier standing in it. Smoke (only the Apocalypse carries it) lays a screen across several tiles that blocks vision for everyone. Force-attacking with Smoke fires one round, then stops. A Move given to a unit already on Force attack here keeps the aim: it drives the course and goes on firing (a Battle Ship can shell a point while it steers clear of submarines), and the aim ends once the point leaves its reach or its guns cannot bear — the blind arc astern on a Battle Ship, the nose arc on a hull gun like the StuG or Jagdtiger. Stop ends it too. 75mm rounds show a tracer in flight. A Tiger can **open the hatch** (I / Scout) so a crewman looks out: the tank sees as far as infantry, including over a rise, but that head can be shot (about 3× trooper HP). A hull hit buttons him up; remaining HP never comes back, and a dead scout means no more hatch. Mixed selections get type tabs there. Shared orders (Stop, Move attack, Patrol, Force attack here, Guard, Rotate, Deploy, Harvest, Sell) sit as quick actions on the bottom left.

Hold **Shift** to queue orders. Each Shift-right-click (move, attack, garrison, repair, supply, board) or Shift-click in Move attack, Force attack here, or Rotate is added behind what the unit is already doing, and the unit carries them out one after another; a group keeps its formation at every stop. Those three modes stay armed while Shift is held, so several points can be clicked in a row. A selected unit shows its queued route as a dashed line: green to walk, red to fight, amber for the rest. Any order given without Shift, or Stop, drops the queue. A point Guard or a Harvest never finishes on its own, so nothing queued after one runs.

**Scrap Yard** (256×256) is the built-in map. It runs dirt lanes from each start into a cross. The **Map Builder** (main menu) makes more: pick a size (48 to 128 cells a side) and max players (2–8), raise, lower, and level the ground, stamp a mountain (a flat cap at its own height, ringed by rock that opens where the ground beside it is raised to that same height), paint grass, road, scrap, water, trees, palms, cacti, fences, and rock, place civilian houses and neutral defences, and set one numbered start per player. **Select** (V) picks up anything placed: drag to move it, R turns it, Delete removes it. On a Watch Tower or a Fire-Control Tower, with the In-game view on, R aims the spotlight and Y sets its sweep. Neutral defences — Bunker, Watch Tower, Sandbags, Barbwire, Dragon's teeth, and Wall — stand on the map for anyone to take: infantry who walk into a bunker or tower make it their side's (a tower can also be captured by standing the capture), and the first side whose men take cover at a sandbag or wall section claims it. Until then nobody shoots at them and a tower's searchlight stays dark. Drag the Sandbags, Barbwire, Dragon's teeth, or Wall tool to lay a line. Barbwire holds infantry and any vehicle rolls it flat; Dragon's teeth stop vehicles and infantry walk between them. Barbwire is a map piece only for now: the engineer does not build it. **Play test** drops you alone onto the sheet as it stands — one start is enough, and nothing is saved; Esc in the fight offers **Return to map builder**. Each start keeps a clear, level pad for the Rig; water sinks to the valley floor and slopes stay walkable. Saved maps are stored on the hub (`gridlock/data/maps`, or `GRIDLOCK_MAPS_DIR`) and appear in every lobby's map list for everyone. Built-in maps cannot be edited; copy one to start from it. Only the browser that saved a map can overwrite or delete it, and a map is locked while a match runs on it. A lobby seats as many commanders as the map has starts, and its preview numbers each start. A start left on **Random** draws one of the free starts at random. Trees each have a few facings and the placer picks one from the tile, so a grove does not all face the same way. The yard has rolling hills and valleys. Units climb slowly and pick cheaper paths around a rise. High ground adds sight and can see the slope below, including each terrace lip. Weapon reach is separate from sight: a rifle stops well inside the fog, a machine gun reaches farther, a scoped rifle farther still, and the Tiger's gun is the longest direct shot — a little past the tank's own eyes, so a spotter is what makes that last band usable. High ground adds a little reach on top of that, not a copy of the wider view. A **Mortarman** kneels, plants the tube, and lobs past every direct-fire gun, over hills and smoke. The round is slow, drifts a little off the aim point, and is devastating to infantry in the open. It will not drop inside a short minimum range. A hit nicks an armored hull, and a tracked tank can lose a track. Accuracy falls off at max range. Ridges block vision past them. Tigers cannot elevate the gun at a steep lip — a hull in a valley or hole cannot hit a target much above it. A hilltop with a clear view fires down; there is no depression limit.

cd gridlock && npm install && npm run dev
```

Open two Chrome tabs to http://localhost:5177

godot --path godot
```

## Playtest

Enter a callsign on first launch (saved locally and skipped next time; also in Options).

**Skirmish** is single-player: click Skirmish from the menu and you land in a solo briefing. No Ready switch, no room code, no join. Esc pauses the fight. The pause menu can save it or load an earlier one, and Load Game on the main menu resumes a save kept in this browser.

**Network:**

1. Network → Create game in one tab.
2. Network → Join game by code in the other.
3. Ready both players; host hits Start.
4. You should see a Rig at each spawn. Hover it — the cursor becomes a gold pointer. Click it twice (or select it and press **E**). A progress bar fills for 3 seconds, then a Core appears. Packing is locked for 2 seconds after that.
