# Sturmgeschütz III Ausf. G (Late) — *Saukopf* mantlet

**Designation:** 7.5 cm Sturmgeschütz 40 Ausf. G (Sd.Kfz. 142/1)  
**Type:** Casemate assault gun / tank destroyer  
**Period:** late 1943 – 1945 (this spec is the late-production look)

This is the late-war StuG: Panzer III hull, sloped casemate, single 80 mm front plate, and a cast *Saukopf* (“sow’s head”) mantlet around a 7.5 cm StuK 40 L/48. It is not a tank with the turret left off. The gun only moves a few degrees, so the whole vehicle has to turn to engage anything off-axis.

---

## Role

Designed in 1936–40 as *Sturmartillerie*: a low, cheap gun that could roll with infantry and smash bunkers, MG nests and field works. After 1941 the short L/24 could not reliably kill T-34s and KVs. From Ausf. F the long StuK 40 turned it into Germany’s main tank destroyer that still carried a useful HE load.

By 1944–45 the late G is an **ambush tank destroyer** issued to Sturmgeschütz brigades and as a stopgap tank in panzer units. About 10,000 StuG III were built in total; Ausf. G is by far the most common.

**Tactical identity**

- Wins from a prepared firing lane and hull-down positions.
- Must hull-steer to engage targets outside a narrow gun arc. Flanking it is the correct counter.
- Lowest German AFV silhouette in its weight class (~2.16 m). Harder to spot than a Panzer IV or T-34.
- Cheap relative to a turreted medium — good as a mass unit.
- Thin roof and sides: artillery airbursts, fighter-bombers and side shots should hurt it more than a medium tank.

**Crew (4)**

| Position | Place |
|---|---|
| Driver | hull, front left |
| Gunner | left of the gun |
| Commander | left rear, cupola |
| Loader (also radio) | right of the gun |

## Build

| | |
|---|---|
| Combat weight | ~23.9 t |
| Length (gun forward) | ~6.77–6.85 m |
| Width | 2.95 m (wider with *Schürzen*) |
| Height | 2.16 m |
| Ground clearance | ~0.39 m |
| Chassis | modified Panzer III, torsion bar, 6 road wheels per side, front sprocket |
| Engine | Maybach HL 120 TRM, water-cooled V12 petrol, **300 PS** at 3,000 rpm |
| Transmission | ZF, 6 forward / 1 reverse |
| Fuel | ~310–320 L petrol |
| Radio | FuG 15 or FuG 16 |

Engine at the rear, fighting compartment in the middle, driver in the left bow. Gun offset slightly right of centre. From Ausf. G the casemate sides are sloped and overhang the tracks.

### Late-G features (this variant)

- Cast *Saukopf* / *Topfblende* mantlet instead of the early-G welded box.
- Two *Saukopf* patterns: gun only, and from about September 1944 a casting with a **coaxial MG 34**.
- Single **80 mm** front plate (not the earlier 50 mm + 30 mm bolted appliqué).
- Commander cupola with **shot deflector** (cupola was a known weak spot until late 1943).
- *Schürzen* — 5 mm side skirts; later vehicles have stronger mounts.
- Roof MG: first a shielded loader-hatch mount, then a **remote-control** MG from spring 1944.
- *Nahverteidigungswaffe* (360° close-defence smoke / HE grenade launcher) on many late vehicles.
- All-steel return rollers.
- **No *Zimmerit*** on the last 1944–45 batches (paste dropped autumn 1944).
- Double-baffle muzzle brake on the L/48.
- Optional *Ostketten* wide tracks in mud / snow.

Sister vehicle: **StuH 42** — same hull, 10.5 cm howitzer, infantry-support specialist.

## Armor

Figures below are the usual **late Ausf. G**. Early A–E are 50 mm front / 30 mm sides. From mid-1942 many vehicles are 50 + 30 mm appliqué; from May 1943 (Alkett) / October 1943 (MIAG) many G’s get a single 80 mm front plate.

| Zone | Thickness | Shape / angle | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hull bow / lower front | **80 mm** | ~21° from vertical | Holds mid-war 75/76 mm APCBC at typical ambush range if the shot is frontal |
| Casemate front | **80 mm** | slight slope (~10°) | Same |
| *Saukopf* mantlet | cast, roughly **50–80 mm**, rounded | no flat face | Extra ricochet chance. Do **not** treat as a flat 80 mm slab. Weak at the gun opening and the join to the casemate |
| Casemate / hull sides | **30 mm** | near-vertical (casemate slightly sloped on G) | The real weakness |
| Hull rear | **50 mm** | slight slope | Better than the sides, still not heavy |
| Casemate rear | **30 mm** | vertical | |
| Roof | **10–17 mm** | nearly flat | Artillery, bombs, aircraft cannon, satchel charges |
| Floor | **15–16 mm** | flat | Mines, very close HE |
| *Schürzen* | **5 mm** spaced | hanging off the sides | Stops AT rifles and some HEAT; not real armour vs AP |

**Effective protection, not raw mm:** 80 mm at 21° is roughly mid-80s to low-90s RHAe vs APCBC. Enough to bounce early T-34 76 mm and Sherman 75 mm at longer range and bad angles. Not enough vs 85 mm, 17-pdr, or a close T-34-85 / IS-2. Sides are 1941 armour on a 1944 battlefield.

### Weak spots

- Ring where the *Saukopf* meets the 80 mm front plate
- Gun barrel and coaxial port (late mantlet)
- Commander’s cupola (better than early G, still thinner than the front plate)
- Driver’s visor / vision slit
- Roof and engine deck
- Lower hull side if *Schürzen* are missing or shot off
- Rear radiator / fuel

## Gun and ammunition

**Main gun:** 7.5 cm StuK 40 L/48 (same family as Panzer IV Ausf. G/H)

| | |
|---|---|
| Traverse | **±10°** manual |
| Elevation | **−6° / +20°** |
| Ammunition | **54** rounds |
| Sight | SflZF1a |

The *Saukopf* does not change the gun arc. Off-axis targets still cost a hull turn.

### Rounds (L/48)

German test figures for **Pzgr. 39 vs plate at 30°**:

| Round | Type | Mass | Muzzle velocity | ~500 m | ~1,000 m | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pzgr. 39** | APCBC-HE | 6.8 kg | ~750–770 m/s | **~91–99 mm** (often cited ~96 mm) | **~81–85 mm** | Standard AP. Explosive filler, good after-armour effect |
| **Pzgr. 40** | APCR (tungsten) | ~4.1 kg | ~930–990 m/s | **~108–126 mm** | falls off fast | Rare. Poor post-penetration. Save for KV / IS / late Sherman |
| **Gr. 38 HL/C** | HEAT | ~4.8 kg | ~450 m/s | **~90–100 mm any range** | same | Low velocity — harder to hit past ~800 m |
| **Sprgr. 34** | HE | 5.74 kg | ~550 m/s | — | — | Infantry, guns, trucks, buildings |

**Suggested rack:** ~45% Pzgr. 39, ~40% HE, a few HEAT, 0–4 APCR. Historically APCR was scarce.

**Practical kill envelope (L/48 + Pzgr. 39)**  
Frontally reliable vs T-34-76, Sherman 75, Cromwell, early KV sides. Marginal vs T-34-85 / late Sherman 76 front. Needs APCR, a flank shot, or HEAT vs heavy tanks. HE still matters — this is not a pure TD like a Nashorn.

Rate of fire: a good crew ~8–10 aimed rounds/min in a static position. Slower than a turreted Panzer IV when the target is off-axis.

**Secondary:** 7.92 mm MG 34 or MG 42, 600 rounds. Late *Saukopf* + remote roof MG is the first StuG that is not helpless against infantry in close. Still worse than a turreted tank.

Same drivetrain family as Panzer III. The *Saukopf* does not add meaningful weight.

|---|---|

| Cross-country | **~20–24 km/h** |
| Power / weight | ~12.5 PS/t |
| Road range | ~155 km |
| Off-road range | ~75–95 km |
| Vertical step | 0.6 m |
| Trench | ~2.3–2.6 m |
| Ford | 0.8 m |
| Gradient | 30° |
| Turn radius | ~5.85 m |
| Ground pressure | ~1.04 kg/cm² |
| Track width | 400 mm (wider with *Ostketten*) |

It keeps up with infantry and Panzer IVs on roads. It is not a chase vehicle. Petrol + rear engine = fire risk if the engine deck is penetrated. Tracks and torsion bars are the usual mobility-kill targets.

## How it should play (Sudden Strike-style)

**Strengths**

- Smallest detection / silhouette of the German medium class
- High frontal bounce vs 75/76 mm, especially on the rounded mantlet
- Mixed AP + HE load — useful in combined arms, not only vs tanks
- Cheap, available in numbers
- Optional *Schürzen* as a module that soaks AT-rifle / HEAT from the side

**Weaknesses that must exist or it becomes a better Panzer IV**

- Gun arc ±10°. Anything outside that forces a hull turn (time + dust + loss of hull-down)
- 30 mm sides. A T-34, Sherman, or even a 57 mm AT gun flanking should be lethal
- Thin roof. Artillery and air should farm unhull-down StuGs
- Petrol fire chance on rear hits

**Encyclopedia line**

> Late StuG III G. Fixed casemate on a Panzer III hull, 80 mm front, cast *Saukopf* around a 7.5 cm L/48. Kills most mediums from the front out to a kilometre; dies the moment someone is on the flank. No turret, 2.16 m high, 24 tonnes.
